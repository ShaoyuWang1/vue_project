Assortment Optimization with Downward Feasibility: Efficient Heuristics Based on Independent Demands

Key words: Assortment optimization, downward feasibility, independent demands, modified revenues

Guillermo Gallego
CUHK(SZ)
Pin Gao
CUHK(SZ)
Shaoyu Wang
CUHK(SZ)
Gerardo Berbegila
The University of Melbourne

A core challenge in revenue management is to select a subset of products or services to offer customers to maximize a firm’s expected revenue. Given the complexity of optimizing assortment decisions under intricate consumer behavior and business constraints, industry practitioners often resort to estimating product demand and optimizing decisions based on the independent demand (IDM) assumption, namely each product’s demand is independent of the other offered products. \ To address its limitation of overlooking substitution effects, this paper proposes an IDM-based optimization variant that aligns “independent” demands with purchase probabilities for single-product offerings and uniformly modifies product revenues by a constant. For last-choice regular models that meet certain bounding conditions, our heuristic achieves the best possible performance guarantees and necessitates solving only two linear programs when the constraints are totally unimodular (TUM) and downward feasible. More generally, we propose heuristic extensions for cases where constraints are not TUM or the bounding conditions are difficult to determine. Beyond its practical simplicity, the proposed methodology also facilitates the development of efficient heuristics for several previously studied problems, either by relaxing assumptions or improving approximation ratios. Extensive computational experiments demonstrate that the proposed heuristics consistently deliver exceptional performance. Furthermore, analysis of a real-world dataset reveals that our heuristics, when derived from models with higher predictive accuracy, achieve superior optimization outcomes.
