The-Data-is-All-You-Need: Model-free Assortment Optimization

Key words: Data-driven, robust optimization;assortment, approximation algorithm, latex styles, author templates

Shaoyu Wang
CUHK(SZ)
Pin Gao
CUHK(SZ)
Andre Cire
Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto
Ningyuan Chen
Rotman School of Management, University of Toronto

Firms use assortment optimization to identify a set of products to offer to consumers in order to maximize the potential revenue. Traditionally, firms first use the historical transaction data to estimate a discrete choice model that predicts the choice behavior of future consumers, and subsequently solve the optimal assortment with such an estimated discrete choice model. This is typically referred to as the predict-then-optimizeapproach. In this paper, we propose an alternative approach that maps the data to the assortment decision in an end-to-end fashion, without the need to estimate a discrete choice model. The formulation extracts the partial orders induced from each customer in the historical transaction data. The assortment is then selected to maximize the worst-case revenue for a new customer when his/her preference is uniformly random, consistent with their partial historical order. We formulate the problem as a mixed-integer program and show its hardness. We also provide two heuristics with tight performance guarantees using LP rounding and revenue-ordered assortments. Our framework is particularly attractive when (1) the data is scarce so that estimating model parameters can be unstable or (2) when the consumer behavior is complex and a chosen discrete choice model is likely to be misspecified.
